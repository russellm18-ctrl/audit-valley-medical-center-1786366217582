## Atmospheric Reality Gap

The four hero images you are running carry a fundamental mismatch with your brand word. "STRENGTH" appears in bold typographic treatment on your homepage, but the visual evidence directly contradicts it. Three of the four images — the mother-and-newborn in a clinical bed, the nurse holding an elderly patient's hands, the solo hiker against a coastal horizon — communicate vulnerability, dependence, and solitude. These are tender moments, not strength. The fourth asset is a text graphic, not a photograph at all. When your copy declares strength and your photography shows fragility and isolation, the visitor's subconscious registers inconsistency before they read a single headline. In healthcare, that gap reads as a brand that doesn't know what it is — and patients choosing between competing regional systems use that signal to self-select away from you.

Supporting that: the hiker image reads as stock — golden-hour backlit male, flannel shirt, coastal overlook. It carries zero geographic or clinical specificity to Renton, Washington, to Valley's cancer expansion, or to any service line. It could belong to a wellness app or an insurance company. It is doing no persuasive work for a patient weighing specialty care choices.

The trust cost: visitors who arrive from branded search already know your name. What they are deciding is whether you feel like the right choice. These images answer that question with softness when your copy is promising strength.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

Your single broken link is your most expensive fracture point. `https://mychart.valleymed.org/` — the MyChart patient portal — is unreachable per the scan. That link appears in your primary navigation and is the first action a returning patient takes. A broken portal link does not read as a technical hiccup; it reads as an organization that does not maintain the tools it hands to patients. A high-value specialist referral landing on your homepage, clicking MyChart to review records before an appointment, and hitting a dead page will call a competitor before they call your IT desk.

Supporting that: the "STRENGTH" typographic image has no photographic context around it on the homepage. As a standalone word-as-image with no scene, no people, no place, it functions as a placeholder, not a brand statement. It signals that a creative asset was needed and a font treatment was used instead.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Your meta description is completely absent. Google currently ranks you #1 organically for "Valley Medical Center" — you own the top position — and yet the snippet that appears beneath your title in search results is being auto-generated by Google from whatever body text it pulls first. That is copy you do not control appearing at the exact moment a prospective patient is deciding whether to click. Your page title "Valley Medical Center & Clinics" at 31 characters is functional but contains no differentiating signal — no location, no specialty anchor, no reason to choose you over a telehealth result below you.

Your `og:image` tag is missing. Every time a link to `valleymed.org` is shared on social media, in a text message, or in an email preview, the card renders with no image — a blank gray box or a broken thumbnail. You are promoting a Phase 1B Cancer Center ribbon-cutting on August 20 and relying on social sharing to drive attendance; without an og:image, every share of that event link looks like a dead URL. One of your 6 homepage content images is also missing alt text (17% gap), which removes that image from screen-reader accessibility and strips it of any image-search indexing value.
